## Why it's interesting

The article claims that fintech engineers who understand both software and financial systems, are in high demand. It also argues that companies should close the talent shortage gap by hiring  more remotely. What interests me the most is how the article is built instead of what it claims. The article was written by an outsourcing company, and its argument ends with an advertisement to their own company which is they are hiring remote engineers. None of the claims were sourced but the only piece point back to the company's own page. As a reader, I was at first very trusting that a medium article would be reliable but now I have to verify each figure myself before trusting it.

The article names the hardest parts of the job which are compliance, high volume transaction systems, and security. Money can't be stored as floating-point numbers without rounding errors. A payment has to be safe to retry without charging someone twice. As someone who recently applied for a fintech related internship for next Summer, I learned that these mechanics matter more than the trends the article emphasizes like AI and blockchain.
